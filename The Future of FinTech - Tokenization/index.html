01 What Is
Tokenization
Distributed Ledger Network Visualization
Definition

From fixed assets to programmable ones.

Tokenization means recording the rights to a real-world asset on a blockchain ledger, so the asset can be split up and moved about as easily as data. A pizza helps here. A $10 million building is a whole pizza, and most people can't afford a whole pizza. Tokenization cuts it into 10,000 slices, and the blockchain is the receipt that shows who owns which slice.

Cryptocurrency created new assets. Tokenization takes ones that already exist (real estate, bonds, gold, private equity) and puts them on-chain. A property deal with a $5M minimum can sell tokens at $1,000 each. Private credit funds that only took institutional money can open to investors anywhere, as long as the smart contract confirms each buyer is allowed to invest.

The closest comparison I can think of is the move from DVDs to streaming. Assets that used to sit in one place, held whole by one owner, start to move like data.

The Legal Wrapper

The token proves ownership on the ledger. The legal wrapper is what a court will recognize. The two have to be tied together, or a transfer on the blockchain means nothing in law.

02
Digital Representation & Compliance

Building the Digital Blueprint

The token is a piece of software that holds the asset's rights. When it uses the ISO-20022 messaging standard, it can exchange messages with banking systems like SWIFT, so both sides can reconcile their records in real time.

  • ·

    ISO-20022 is the message format banks use for payments. A token that uses it can talk to bank systems without a custom translation layer.

  • ·

    SWIFT is the network banks use to send payment instructions worth trillions of dollars a day. Tokens that use ISO-20022 can connect to it without anyone reconciling records by hand.

Key Information

Asset type, who issued it, and an on-chain ID that no other token shares.

Embedded Rights

Voting rights, interest payment schedules, and dividend payouts, written into the token's code.

Compliance Rules

Transfer restrictions for each jurisdiction, such as accredited-investor requirements, enforced automatically by the token.

Provenance

A full history of every owner. Nobody can edit it, and anyone with access to the ledger can audit it.

03
The Logic Layer

Smart Contracts as Execution Engine

A smart contract is code that runs on its own when set conditions are met, so nobody has to approve each step by hand. Tokens on the same network share that code, which means they can be combined: you could put up tokenized gold as collateral for a loan, earn yield on a tokenized bond, and settle in a stablecoin, all in one transaction.

Atomic Settlement

The asset and the payment change hands at the same moment. If the payment isn't there, the asset doesn't move, so one side can't pay and then never receive anything.

bolt

Automation of Dividends

Rent or interest goes to token holders as soon as it comes in, without a fund administrator in the middle.

balance

Real-Time Reconciliation

The contract updates the books with every transaction, so there are no overnight batches and no month-end scramble to make the records match.

shield

Compliance Enforcement

A trade that violates jurisdiction rules or Know Your Customer and Anti-Money Laundering requirements is rejected before it executes.

04
Immobilization & Asset Locking

Solving the Double-Spend Problem

The physical asset has to be locked up so nobody can sell it offline while its token trades online. In reference-backed tokenization, a gold bar sits in a vault and the token is the only way to trade it.

On-Chain Issuance

Some bonds are issued directly on the blockchain and have no physical counterpart, so there is nothing to lock in a vault. The ledger entry is the bond.

Bankruptcy Remoteness

In places without clear digital-asset laws, the asset behind a token can get lumped in with a bankrupt issuer's other property. A bankruptcy-remote structure keeps the asset with the token holders instead of the company's creditors.

05
Minting, Distribution & Lifecycle

The Token Goes Live

Minting creates the tokens on the ledger. They go to investors' wallets, trade around the clock, and collect coupon payments automatically. At maturity the token is burned and the holder receives the underlying value. Right now the market runs on two kinds of network.

Permissioned Networks

Private consortium ledgers that banks prefer for privacy, such as JPMorgan's Kinexys, Goldman's DAP, and HSBC Orion.

Permissionless Networks

Public networks such as Ethereum, Polygon, and Avalanche, open to capital from anywhere. They are now growing faster than permissioned networks.

Chronology

The Tokenization
Timeline

2015

Ethereum Launches

Smart contracts make it possible, at least in theory, to tokenize real-world assets.

2017

First STOs Emerge

Security Token Offerings give issuers a way to tokenize securities and equity stakes within existing securities law.

2020

DeFi Summer

Decentralized Finance protocols run automated lending and yield payments at scale, showing the technology can handle real money.

2022

Institutional Entry

JPMorgan's Onyx runs tokenized repo trades for institutional clients, and Goldman Sachs readies its Digital Asset Platform (DAP), which goes live in early 2023. These are the first serious experiments by big banks.

2023-24

BlackRock Arrives

BlackRock's Blockchain USD Institutional Digital Liquidity fund (BUIDL) launches in March 2024 and crosses $500M in under four months. Franklin Templeton, Ondo, and Centrifuge issue billions between them, and tokenized Treasuries pass $1 billion. The EU, UAE, and Singapore publish clear rules.

2025

The GENIUS Act

The US passes its first federal rules for stablecoins, easing the compliance worries that had kept the largest pools of capital on the sidelines.

2026

Clarity Act Stalls

The Clarity Act, a broader federal framework for digital assets, passed the House in July 2025 but fell one vote short of advancing in the Senate in September 2026. Tokenized real-world assets reach about $39 billion on-chain.

2030 ↗

$2T to $16T

McKinsey's base case is about $2 trillion. BCG projects up to $16 trillion, roughly 10% of global GDP. By then the line between traditional finance and DeFi may be hard to find.

Outlook

The Road Ahead

What's Still in the Way

Percentages: share of 300 financial institutions naming each risk in Broadridge's 2025 Tokenization Survey

Regulatory Uncertainty

73%

Unclear or insufficient regulation is the barrier institutions name most often. A tokenized bond that is legal in Singapore may be illegal to sell in Germany, and until countries line up their rules, room for regulatory arbitrage and systemic risk both keep growing.

Technology & Cybersecurity Risk

54%

Code has bugs, and bugs get exploited. If a contract managing $200M of tokenized real estate is hacked, the losses land on people who own pieces of real buildings.

Legacy System Integration

Connecting decades-old back-office systems to blockchain ledgers is hard technical work. It won't happen overnight, and building the bridge costs a lot.

Thin Secondary Trading

Many projects never get past the pilot stage. In RWA.xyz data, 56% of tokenized asset value saw no transfers at all in a given week. It's a cold-start problem: issuers won't tokenize without buyers, and buyers won't show up without trading volume.

What's Pushing It Forward

+

Legislative Clarity

The GENIUS Act (2025) gave the US clear rules for stablecoins, which removes one of the main reasons large institutions stayed out. The Clarity Act would do the same for the rest of the market, but it stalled in the Senate in September 2026.

+

Cross-Network Interoperability

Swift is testing transfers between blockchains with Chainlink and banks including BNY Mellon and Citi. Separate blockchains only add up to one market once something connects them.

+

Settlement Infrastructure

With ISO-20022, bank messaging systems and blockchains can exchange messages directly, which connects tokenized markets to the trillions of dollars in daily payments that run over SWIFT.

+

The $20B Savings Argument

A GFMA and BCG study estimates that distributed ledgers could save about $20 billion a year in global clearing and settlement costs. Savings that large are hard to treat as an experiment, and banks that wait will be stuck with higher costs than the ones that move.

Editorial View

Paper-heavy, siloed systems are on their way out. I think the institutions that do well over the next decade will be the ones that build compliance in from the start and make their assets work across networks, so capital moves about as easily as data. The infrastructure scales now, stablecoins finally have US rules, and the big incumbents are putting real money in. The rest of the legislation is still stuck, but I don't have much doubt that this happens. What I'm less sure of is who builds the infrastructure, because whoever does gets to decide who benefits.
